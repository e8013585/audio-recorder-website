Privacy Policy

For the Audio Recorder Android application.
Last updated: 2026-08-15

In short: your recordings stay on your device. This app has no accounts and no analytics, and it never uploads your audio anywhere. Three things do involve a third party, and each is described below: the app shows a banner advert served by Google, optional location tagging uses Google's location and place-name services, and Android's own backup can copy the app's settings and recording list to your Google account.

Who we are

This application is developed by Hasan B. & Levent B. ("we", "us"). If you have questions about this policy, contact us at .

Information we collect

We do not collect any personal information, and we never collect your recordings. We operate no servers that receive your data, and the app contains no analytics or crash reporting libraries.

The app does show advertising, which is provided by Google AdMob. Google receives some device information in order to serve those ads, and the consent service that decides whether a consent form is needed is contacted when the app starts. That is described in Advertising below. Your recordings are never used for advertising and are never uploaded.

Two other features involve data leaving your device, both covered in their own sections: location tagging, which asks Google's services to turn your coordinates into a place name, and Android's backup system, which can copy the app's settings and recording list to your own Google account.

Everything the app creates is stored on your device:

  • Audio recordings, saved to your device's Recordings folder.
  • Recording details such as name, duration, size, format and waveform, held in a database on your device.
  • Your settings, including audio preferences, appearance and language.
  • An optional PIN, stored only as a salted cryptographic hash in encrypted storage. It is never stored as readable text and cannot be recovered from the device.
  • A place name for the start and end of each recording, if location tagging is on and you have granted the location permission.

Uninstalling the app removes its database and settings from the device. Recordings saved to the shared Recordings folder remain on your device unless you delete them, and any copy Android has already placed in your Google account backup is removed through your Google account rather than by uninstalling — see Backup and device transfer.

Location tagging

When location tagging is on, the app records an approximate place name at the start and end of each recording. Two steps are involved, and you should know that neither is purely on-device:

  • The coarse position comes from Google Play services' location provider, which may use nearby Wi-Fi networks and mobile masts, and Google's network location service, to work out where the device is.
  • Those coordinates are then turned into a place name by Android's geocoding service, which on most phones is provided by Google and requires a network connection. In other words, your approximate coordinates are sent to Google to get the name back.

Only the resulting text — a city and country or US state — is kept, in the app's local database. Latitude and longitude are never stored, and nothing about your location is sent to us. Google's handling of what it receives is covered by the Google Privacy Policy.

Location tagging is on by default in the app's settings, but it can do nothing until you grant the location permission, which the app asks for the first time you record. Declining that prompt, or turning the setting off under Settings → Recording, stops all of the above. Place names already saved with earlier recordings stay until you delete those recordings.

Backup and device transfer

The app takes part in Android's standard backup system, which is a feature of your device and your Google account rather than a service of ours. If you have backup enabled in your Android settings, Android may copy the following to your Google account, and will copy it during a device-to-device transfer:

  • Your app settings and preferences.
  • The recordings database — names, dates, durations, formats, waveforms and any place names. This is information about your recordings, not the audio.

Audio files are not part of this. They live in the shared Recordings folder, which the app's backup rules do not include. Your PIN and the file server's credentials are explicitly excluded from both backup and device transfer.

This data goes to your own Google account, not to us; we cannot see it. Android backups are encrypted, and on recent versions are protected by your device's PIN or passcode. You can turn backup off, or delete an existing backup, in your device's settings under Google → Backup.

The Wi-Fi file server

The app includes an optional server that lets you browse and download your recordings from a web browser on the same local network. It is off by default and runs only while you switch it on. It is read-only: it can list your recordings and send you a copy of one, and there is no way to upload, rename or delete anything through it. It is reachable only from your local network — it does not expose anything to the internet, and no recording is sent to us or to any third party.

Access requires a username and password that the app generates randomly and stores in encrypted storage. Be aware that the connection is ordinary HTTP, not HTTPS: the credentials and the audio are not encrypted in transit, so anyone able to observe traffic on that network could read them. Only enable the server on networks you trust, and switch it off when you have finished.

Advertising

The app displays a single banner advert. Adverts are served by Google AdMob, and Google acts as an independent data controller for what it collects. To serve adverts, Google may receive:

  • Your device's advertising ID, a resettable identifier you control in your device settings.
  • Standard technical information such as device model, operating system version, coarse region and IP address.
  • Whether an advert was shown, viewed or tapped.
  • On devices that support Android's Privacy Sandbox, the ads SDK may also use the system's Topics and attribution services. These are Android features: the topics are worked out by your device from your app usage generally, not by this app, and you can review or disable them in your device's settings under Privacy → Ads.

We do not send Google your recordings, file names, transcripts, location tags, or anything else the app stores. We receive only aggregate, anonymous performance figures, and we cannot identify you from them.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the app asks for your consent before any personalised advert is requested, and no advert is loaded until you have answered. You can change your answer at any time from Settings → Ads → Ad privacy choices; that entry is shown only where a consent choice applies, so you will not see it in regions where Google reports that none is required.

Handling consent means the app contacts Google's User Messaging Platform when it starts, to find out whether a form is needed and what you have already answered. That request happens before any advert is loaded and tells Google only what it needs for that decision, such as the app's identifier and the region your connection appears to come from. If it cannot reach that service, the app loads no adverts at all rather than guessing at your choice.

You can reset or delete your advertising ID from your device's settings, under Privacy or Google → Ads. Deleting it stops adverts from being personalised. Google's own handling of this data is covered by the Google Privacy Policy.

Sharing

We do not sell your information, and we cannot share what we never receive. The one thing that is shared is the advertising identifier described above, which the ads SDK sends to Google; Google is an independent controller for it. Nothing else — no audio, no file names, no place names, no settings — is shared with anyone.

When you use the share function, your recording is passed to whichever app you choose, and that app's own privacy policy then applies to it. The same is true of anything you copy off the device yourself through the Wi-Fi server.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13. Because we collect no personal information from anyone, we do not knowingly collect it from children.

Security

Recordings are written to your device's storage and are protected by your device's own security. The optional app lock adds a PIN or biometric check on top. PINs are salted and hashed with PBKDF2 (65,536 iterations) and kept in encrypted storage, along with the Wi-Fi server's credentials; neither is included in any backup. A PIN cannot be recovered from the device, so if you forget it you will need to clear the app's data.

The app lock protects the app's own screens. It does not encrypt the audio files themselves, which remain readable to anything else on your device with storage access.

Your rights and choices

You are in control of everything the app stores: delete individual recordings from the list, turn location tagging off at any time under Settings → Recording, revoke the location or microphone permission in Android's settings, remove your PIN in settings, turn the Wi-Fi server off, change your advertising consent where that choice applies, disable Android backup for the app, or uninstall the app to remove its data from the device. Since we hold no data about you, there is nothing for you to request from us; requests about data held by Google should go to Google.
